# Relationship Coach Micro-Actions Library

**Purpose:** Tiny, specific actions (2-5 minutes) that build connection without solving the underlying issue.

**Format:** Always use `_Try: [action]._` in italics when offering in conversation.

---

## Connection Repair

### Appreciation
• Send a 2-line appreciation text naming one specific thing you liked from today.
• Text them one thing they did this week that made you smile (even something tiny).
• Say "thank you for [specific action]" out loud — no explanation needed, just that.
• Write down three things you appreciate about them. Don't send it. Just sit with what shifts.
• At dinner tonight, name one thing you noticed they did well today.

### Presence
• Sit together for 10 minutes without phones, agenda, or solving anything.
• Hold hands for 2 minutes in silence before bed tonight.
• Ask if they want company while they [task]. Just be there, no talking required.
• Make eye contact for 30 seconds without looking away or laughing (it'll be awkward — that's okay).
• Sit close enough that your shoulders touch for the length of one song.

### Vulnerability
• Share one small thing you're nervous about this week (unrelated to your conflict).
• Say "I miss you" out loud without explaining what went wrong.
• Tell them one thing you wish they knew about how you're feeling — in one sentence.
• Share one moment from today when you felt lonely, even if they were there.
• Name one thing you're afraid of about your relationship. Then pause. No fixing.

## Communication

### Questions (Curiosity-Driven)
• Ask one curious "how" or "what" question about their day and give 30 seconds of quiet after they answer.
• Ask "what surprised you today?" and listen without offering your own answer yet.
• Ask "how are you feeling about us right now?" — then wait, even if silence feels heavy.
• Ask "what's one thing I don't know about what you're carrying this week?"
• Say "tell me more" three times in your next conversation. Just those three words.

### Listening
• When they talk tonight, don't interrupt, fix, or relate it to yourself. Just listen for 5 minutes.
• Repeat back one thing they said in your own words: "I hear you saying [X]. Is that right?"
• Put your phone face-down and give them eye contact for your whole next conversation.
• After they share something hard, pause 10 seconds before responding. Let it land first.
• Ask "is there more?" after they finish talking. Sometimes there is.

### Boundaries
• Share one boundary in one sentence; offer one alternative that still honors it.
• Name one "I need [X]" statement. Don't justify or apologize for it.
• Say "I can't do [X] right now, but I can do [Y]. Would that work?"
• Tell them one thing you need space from this week — then offer one thing you want to stay close to.
• Say "I need 10 minutes to myself before we talk about this." Then take it.

## Intimacy

### Physical Connection
• Hold hands for 2 minutes in silence before bed.
• Hug for 20 seconds (longer than feels natural). No agenda, just the hug.
• Sit close enough that your legs touch while you watch [show/movie/nothing].
• Give them a 2-minute shoulder rub without expecting it back.
• Kiss them goodbye in the morning like you mean it — 5 full seconds, not a peck.

### Emotional Sharing
• Share one thing that surprised you about them when you first met.
• Tell them one way they've changed you for the better — be specific.
• Say "I feel [emotion] when [specific situation]" without blaming or solving.
• Share one fear you have about losing them. Then let them sit with it.
• Tell them one thing you hope for your future together (even if it feels uncertain right now).

### Playfulness
• Send them a meme or song that reminds you of an inside joke you share.
• Ask "if we had one free day together with no agenda, what would you want to do?"
• Share one silly thing that happened today — something that made you laugh or roll your eyes.
• Do one small thing they love that you usually resist (their favorite takeout, their show, etc.).
• Text them "I'm thinking about [specific memory]" — something tender or funny you shared.

## Repair After Conflict

### Apology & Acknowledgment
• Say "I'm sorry for [specific action], not for how you felt about it" — own your part, nothing more.
• Text "I miss you" without explaining what went wrong. Just those three words.
• Say "I hear that I hurt you when I [specific action]. That wasn't my intent, and I'm sorry."
• Acknowledge one thing they said during the fight that was actually true, even if it stung.
• Say "I don't want to fight. Can we start over?" Then wait for their answer.

### De-escalation
• Take 3 slow breaths together before continuing a hard conversation.
• Say "I need a 10-minute break. I'll come back." Then actually come back.
• Lower your voice and slow your words for one full sentence. See if they match you.
• Say "I'm feeling [emotion]. Can we pause and come back to this when we're both calmer?"
• Touch their hand or shoulder for 5 seconds without saying anything. Just reconnect.

### Moving Forward
• Ask "what's one thing I could do differently next time this happens?"
• Say "I want to repair this. What do you need from me right now?"
• Suggest one small ritual to end fights (e.g., "let's always end with a hug, even if we're still mad").
• Name one thing you both did well during the conflict (e.g., "we stayed" or "we didn't yell").
• Ask "are we okay?" Then listen to the whole answer, even if it's not "yes" yet.

## Solo Actions (When Partner Isn't Available/Ready)

### Self-Reflection
• Write down one thing you love about them. Don't send it. Just notice how it shifts what you carry.
• List three things they've done for you in the past month. Sit with gratitude before solving anything.
• Journal: "What am I really needing right now that I haven't named yet?"
• Notice one pattern you bring to conflicts. Not to fix it, just to see it.
• Ask yourself: "Am I trying to be right, or trying to be close?" Write the answer.

### Self-Soothing
• Take 5 slow breaths and name what you're feeling out loud (even to an empty room).
• Text a friend: "I'm struggling with [X]. Just needed to say it out loud. No advice needed."
• Do one thing that grounds you (walk, music, shower, tea) before re-engaging.
• Write them a letter you'll never send. Get it all out, then burn or delete it.
• Say this out loud: "I'm doing the best I can. So are they." Even if it doesn't feel true yet.

### Preparation for Reconnection
• Identify one thing you want from the next conversation (connection, clarity, peace, etc.).
• Practice saying one hard thing in one sentence. Then practice saying it softer.
• Decide on one boundary you need to hold, and one thing you're willing to flex on.
• Plan one small gesture of reconnection (text, touch, question) before diving into "the talk."
• Ask yourself: "What would repair look like, even if we don't solve the issue today?"

## Micro-Action Dos & Don'ts

### DO:
✅ Make actions **concrete** ("Send a 2-line text..." not "reach out")
✅ Include **timing** ("10 minutes", "2 lines", "30 seconds")
✅ Keep actions **low-stakes** (safe to try even when anxious)
✅ Focus on **connection over solution** (builds bridge, doesn't fix issue)
✅ Use italics format: `_Try: [action]._`

### DON'T:
❌ Offer multiple options (creates decision paralysis)
❌ Make actions vague ("communicate better")
❌ Require partner's cooperation if they're unavailable
❌ Suggest actions that need hours or planning
❌ Skip the micro-action in favor of just talking

---

## Usage Notes

- **One per response**: Never offer more than one micro-action at a time
- **Context-match**: Pick actions that fit the user's situation (repair vs. routine check-in)
- **Adapt language**: Use user's own words when framing the action
- **Honor readiness**: If user isn't ready for action, validate first, offer action next turn

---

**Remember:** Micro-actions are about **tending connection**, not solving problems. They're tiny stitches that hold hearts together while bigger repairs happen over time.
